Rules for sent emails in new Outlook: every option that works

Classic Outlook had a rule type called "Apply rule on messages I send." New Outlook doesn't — its rules act on received mail only, and Microsoft hasn't committed to a date for bringing the sent side back. If you filed sent mail into client or project folders, that system broke the day you were migrated. Here is the full map of what still works, including the ways that cost nothing. One of the options is ours, and we say so where it appears.

What actually happened

In classic Outlook for Windows, the Rules wizard could start from "Apply rule on messages I send" — file a copy of outgoing mail by recipient, keep sent and received mail for a project in one folder, or drop a copy of everything you send into your Inbox for follow-up. In new Outlook and Outlook on the web, rules trigger only on messages you receive. There is no equivalent setting, no hidden toggle, and no server-side rule that files into folders. If you went looking and couldn't find it: you didn't miss a menu. It isn't there.

You're also not alone. The public question threads about this on Microsoft's own support forums have accumulated dozens of "same question" votes and years of follow-ups, and community MVPs maintain long-running workaround guides because the searches never stop.

The four options, with their real costs

Free · works today

1. Stay on classic Outlook

Classic Outlook still has the sent-mail rule, and if you have the choice, keeping it is a perfectly reasonable answer — plenty of people have rolled back for exactly this reason.

!

Classic runs on Microsoft's retirement clock, not yours. Migrations to new Outlook are rolling through organizations and personal machines on an ongoing basis, and when yours lands, this option ends with no appeal.

Free · the community workaround

2. CC or BCC yourself, then file the copy

The workaround the community has carried for years, and it genuinely works:

  1. On a message you want filed, add your own address to CC or BCC.
  2. In new Outlook, create an ordinary incoming rule: from you (or matching a keyword you include) → move to the folder you want.
  3. The copy that arrives back gets filed by that rule.
!

You keep a duplicate of every message — the filed copy is the one that arrived, while the original still sits in Sent Items. With CC, your recipients can see you copying yourself. And in new Outlook there's no automatic way to add the CC/BCC, so it's a per-message habit you have to keep.

Free-ish · for flow builders

3. Power Automate

If you have a Microsoft 365 work account, Power Automate can watch your Sent Items and move messages matching conditions into folders. It's real, and for one or two simple patterns it can be enough.

!

You're building and babysitting a flow per pattern — connections expire, flows silently suspend, and every new client folder means editing automation. It's a hobby project wearing a filing system's clothes, and it's generally not available on personal outlook.com accounts.

Questions people actually ask

Can new Outlook apply rules to sent messages at all?

No. Rules in new Outlook and Outlook on the web run on received mail only. "Apply rule on messages I send" exists in classic Outlook and nowhere else, and Microsoft hasn't committed to a date for the sent side in new Outlook.

Will Microsoft bring it back?

They haven't said no forever — they've said classic-only, with no committed date, for several years running. Plan around what exists, not the backlog.

Does SentRules copy or move the sent message?

It moves. One copy, in the folder your rule names, wearing a "Filed by SR" category. If your system depends on everything also staying in Sent Items, the CC-yourself workaround fits you better today — that's the honest answer.

Does it work on a plain personal outlook.com account?

Yes. Personal outlook.com and Microsoft 365 alike, and sends from your phone or the web file exactly like desktop sends, because filing happens server-side after the send.

What happens when no rule matches?

Nothing — deliberately. Your sent copy stays in Sent Items, untouched. SentRules never guesses a folder, and conflicting rules leave the copy where it is and tell you, instead of flipping a coin with your mail.
